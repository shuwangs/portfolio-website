> 🌱 Before I get into it, here’s how I’ve been feeling lately. The more I learn, the more I realize how little I know.

I was in a coding interview for an SDET role when the interviewer asked, “Have you ever put together a test plan?”
My first thought was, *Oh no. Brain, please don’t leave the meeting.* 🫠 “Test plan” was a term I rarely used. I’d just explained the kinds of tests I’d worked on, but had I ever put together a test plan? Honestly, no. I was crying on the inside. 😭

> Afterward, I thought about how familiar the idea actually was. When I travel, I make a plan. When I worked in a lab, I decided what question I wanted to answer, what controls I needed, and what results would tell me whether the experiment worked.

Testing software raises similar questions. What do I need to verify? What cases should I cover first? What would a passing result look like?

> 💡 I laughed a little when I made the connection. I’d spent years planning experiments, and there I was, thrown off by the words “test plan.”

Okay, my tiny story grew legs and became a whole introduction. Back to the notes. 🐾

## 📝 What is a Test Plan?
A test plan is a document detailing the objectives, resources, and processes for a specific test session for a software or hardware product. The plan typically contains a detailed understanding of the eventual workflow. --- From Wikipedia


## 🧩 How to Create a Test Plan
- 🎯 Define the goal and scope
- 🗓️ Schedule timelines
- 🔎 Define test objectives
- 🛠️ Design test strategies
- 📦 Determine test deliverables
- 🧪 Plan the test environment and test data
  
It may vary depending on your project size or your team's requirements.

> 🐣 Note to future me: start with what needs testing and why. Give the plan a shape, then fill in the details.
